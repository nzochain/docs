Token approvals are on-chain permissions that let a **spender contract** move specific tokens from your wallet. Many dApps request **unlimited** approvals that stay active until you revoke them. That is the main surface for wallet drainers after phishing or a compromised protocol.

NZOChain **reads** approvals across **Ethereum, BNB Chain, Polygon, and Arbitrum** and flags risk as part of scans and the [RiskScore](/features/risk-score).

## What we analyze

| Signal | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|
| Unlimited allowance | Spender can drain the full token balance without asking again |
| Spender reputation | Heuristics + multi-source signals (rules engine, denylists, proprietary triage) |
| Approval age | Old approvals to unused contracts increase silent exposure |
| Connected dApps | Inferred from active approvals — which protocols still can move tokens |
| Cross-chain exposure | Same wallet on multiple chains may have different approval sets |

Results are **advisory**. They are not a guarantee that a spender is safe or malicious.

## In the B2C app

- **Dashboard / Approvals**: list active permissions, risk hints, and (on eligible plans) revoke actions you confirm in your wallet.
- **Scan on-demand** and **opt-in 6-hour re-scans** refresh approval state; this is **not** real-time mempool monitoring.

To **revoke**, you always sign in your own wallet — non-custodial. Step-by-step: [Permission revoke](/features/permission-revoke).

## After a suspected hack

Use [Forensic analysis — Foste hackeado?](/features/forensic-analysis) for a 90-day timeline and verdict triage. Residual approvals load in the forensic flow **after login**; revocation still requires wallet connect.
